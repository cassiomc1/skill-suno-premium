# Songwriting, Earworm Craft & Lyric Structure Reference

This reference governs how songs are conceived, structured, phrased, polished for AI vocals, and kept original across repeated sessions.

---

## 1. The 8 Laws of Addictive Songcraft (Pop, Electronic, Rock, Latin, Contemporary)

Apply these when writing hook-driven songs (for `aaba`, `blues12`, `through_composed`, or classical/jazz forms, see Form-Aware Contracts below):

1. **Hook Gravity**: The chorus hook is **2–7 words, 4–9 syllables**, phonetically sticky. Favor open vowels (`oh`, `ah`, `ay`, `ee`) and crisp consonants that punch the kick/snare. The hook must still work over a bare acoustic or minimal arrangement.
2. **Title = Hook Seed**: The title is **1–4 words**—either the hook itself or a sharp concrete image the hook orbits. Never a genre label or vague abstraction.
3. **Tension → Gap → Payoff**: Build pressure through the pre-chorus, insert a 1-beat `(hold silence)` or filter cutoff right before the chorus/drop, then release maximum emotional weight. Anticipation triggers stronger listener payoff than continuous loudness.
4. **Purposeful Repetition with Escalation**: Repeat the central refrain, but **never copy-paste three identical choruses**. Each return adds one layer via performance cues—`(harmonies)`, `(stacked doubles)`, `(octave lift)`, `(crowd chant)`, `(counter-melody)`, `(ad-lib)`. Verses must always advance the story; never loop verse wording or recycle the same rhyme sound across every verse.
5. **Verse Specificity (Camera Shots)**: Every verse line is a concrete camera shot—a specific place, physical sensation, object, gesture, or consequence. Ban abstract filler.
6. **One Unforgettable Image**: Anchor the song in one precise image born directly from the user's story. Change the setting, action, and sensory palette between songs; never recycle a stock object.
7. **Prosody**: Align naturally stressed syllables with strong beats (kick/snare). Use shorter, punchier words on drops and rhythmic peaks.
8. **Final Payoff (+20% Bigger)**: The final chorus/drop must deliver a clear lift—extra harmonies, stacked doubles, choir/crowd response, or one fresh resolving line that completes the narrative arc.

## 2. Writing Modes: Classic vs. ReMi

Both are creative writing directions applied before drafting:

- **Classic Mode**: Clear structure, natural conversational phrasing, immediate singability, and a memorable central motif. Each verse introduces a clear new action or consequence while honoring the chosen musical form.
- **ReMi Mode (Bold / Unpredictable)**: Less predictable phrasing, surprising yet coherent imagery associations, varied sentence openings and rhyme sounds, sharper emotional contrasts, and unexpected lyrical turns—while remaining effortlessly singable and faithful to the user's premise and form.

## 3. Divergent Planning: 3-Candidate Concept Architect + Craft Rotation

Before drafting lyrics for any new full song:

1. **Pick a Rotating Craft Pair** (adapt to the user's actual story; never replace their premise):
   - **8 Narrative Viewpoints**:
     1. A decision and its consequence
     2. A discovery that changes the opening scene
     3. Two perspectives on the same event
     4. An ordinary action carrying unexpected meaning
     5. A question answered differently by the end
     6. A small event told through its aftermath
     7. A journey with a surprising destination
     8. A promise tested by a concrete action
   - **8 Observational Lenses**:
     1. Texture and movement
     2. Distance and sound
     3. Physical gestures
     4. Changes in light and material
     5. Timing and interruptions
     6. Contrasts of scale
     7. Spatial relationships
     8. Unexpected but plausible associations
2. **Generate 3 Distinct Concept Candidates** internally, each with 6 compact fields (`concept`, `title_angle`, `hook_angle`, `sonic_twist`, `lyric_rule`, `musical_development`). `title_angle` and `hook_angle` use the target lyric language; the remaining fields use English.
3. **Select the Sharpest, Most Novel Candidate**: Reject any candidate that reuses recent titles or leans on generic default props (coffee cups, kettles, porch lights, old radios, empty streets, generic stars/cosmos—use such objects only if the user's actual story calls for them).

## 4. Form-Aware Arrangement Contracts (All 10 Forms)

Every section tag **must stay in English** (`[Intro]`, `[Verse 1]`, `[Chorus]`, etc.) even when sung lines are in Portuguese, Spanish, French, Arabic, or another language. Every bracketed section must be populated—never leave empty headers. Verses and choruses must contain actual sung lines, not only parenthesized cues.

| Arrangement ID | Structure Contract | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `radio` | `[Intro]` → `[Verse 1]` → (optional `[Pre-Chorus]`) → `[Chorus]`/`[Drop]` → contrast (`[Verse 2]`, `[Bridge]`) → `[Final Chorus]`/`[Final Drop]` → `[Outro]` | Complete ~3 min arc with concise intro and resolved ending. |
| `extended` | Electronic: `[DJ Intro]` → `[Intro]` → `[Verse 1]` → `[Pre-Chorus]` → `[Chorus]` → `[Drop]` → `[Verse 2]` → `[Build-up]` → `[Chorus]` → `[Drop]` → `[Bridge]` → `[Final Drop]` → `[DJ Outro]`. Non-electronic: full song with extended instrumental passages and resolved ending (no DJ intro/drop). | Respect genre family! |
| `beatport_club` | `[DJ Intro]` → `[Intro]` → `[Verse 1]` → `[Pre-Chorus]` → `[Chorus]` → `[Drop]` → `[Breakdown]` → `[Build-up]` → `[Final Drop]` → `[DJ Outro]` | Club mix with mix-in/mix-out percussion space. |
| `full_8min` | Long-form journey with multiple contrasting passages, builds/drops (electronic) or extended solo/ensemble developments (non-electronic), and a resolved `[Outro]`/`[DJ Outro]`. | Up to ~8 min capacity on Suno v6. |
| `tiktok` | **Open immediately** with `[Chorus]`, `[Drop]`, `[Build]`, or `[Pre-Chorus]` (central hook inside the first 8 bars) → `[Outro]`. | **No slow `[Intro]` or `[Verse]` before the hook.** |
| `acoustic` | Full song arc (`[Intro]` → `[Verse 1]` → `[Chorus]` → contrast → `[Final Chorus]` → `[Outro]`) with intimate acoustic dynamics. | No mandatory electronic build or drop. |
| `live` | Full song arc with responsive live-ensemble interplay, call-and-response, and clear concert ending. | No mandatory electronic drop. |
| `aaba` | `[Verse 1]` → `[Verse 2]` → `[Bridge]` → `[Verse 3]` → `[Outro]` | Contrasting `[Bridge]` (B section); `[Verse 3]` reprises/resolves the A theme. **No mandatory pop chorus or drop.** |
| `blues12` | `[Verse 1]` → `[Verse 2]` → `[Verse 3]` → `[Outro]` | Each verse is an **AAB lyric stanza** over a 12-bar `I–IV–V` cycle with instrumental responses. **No mandatory pop chorus or drop.** |
| `through_composed` | `[Verse 1]` → `[Verse 2]` → `[Verse 3]` → `[Outro]` | Continuously developing contrasting sung themes; **no mandatory repeated chorus or drop.** |

### Special Workflow Contracts
- **`instrumental` mode**: `Lyrics` **must be completely empty (`""`)**. Develop motifs, contrasts, and resolution exclusively inside `Style`.
- **`extend` / `replace_section`**: Write **only** the requested continuation or replacement sections with populated English section tags. Preserve the existing track's key, BPM, and identity; never restart the song from `[Intro]`.
- **`sounds`**: Short sample/texture `Style` only; `Lyrics` empty (or optional minimal vocal texture).

## 5. Suno Metatags & Performance Cue Vocabulary

### Reliable Section Tags (Bracketed, English Only)
`[Intro]`, `[DJ Intro]`, `[Verse 1]`, `[Verse 2]`, `[Verse 3]`, `[Pre-Chorus]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Drop]`, `[Build]`, `[Build-up]`, `[Breakdown]`, `[Bridge]`, `[Instrumental Break]`, `[Final Chorus]`, `[Final Drop]`, `[Outro]`, `[DJ Outro]`

### Reliable Performance Cues (Parenthesized, English Only)
Place on their own line or directly after a lyric line:
`(whispered)`, `(breathy)`, `(spoken)`, `(close mic)`, `(falsetto)`, `(harmonies)`, `(stacked doubles)`, `(call and response)`, `(crowd chant)`, `(distant reverb vocal)`, `(ad-lib)`, `(build intensity)`, `(hold silence)`

For instrumental breaks/breakdowns, include a short production cue in parentheses rather than leaving the section empty—e.g.:
```text
[Instrumental Break]
(warm Rhodes solo over locked bass groove, crisp hi-hat fills)
```

## 6. Dynamic Energy Maps (0–10 Scale)

Plan section-by-section energy contrast before writing performance cues. Apply the intensity bias (`subtle`: `-2`, `building`: `0`, `peak` / `raw` / `dopamine`: `+1`; quiet valleys ≤ 4 shift by at most ±1 so peaks pull further away from valleys):

- **Radio Base Arc**: `Intro(2) → Verse 1(5) → Pre-Chorus(7) → Chorus(9) → Verse 2(5) → Pre-Chorus(7) → Chorus(9) → Bridge(4) → Final Chorus(10) → Outro(3)`
- **Club / Beatport Base Arc**: `DJ Intro(3) → Intro Swell(4) → Verse 1(5) → Pre-Chorus(7) → Chorus(9) → Drop(9) → Breakdown(4) → Build-up(8) → Final Drop(10) → DJ Outro(3)`
- **TikTok / Short Clip Base Arc**: `Build-up(7) → Drop(10) → Chorus(9) → Outro(3)`
- **Full 8-Min Base Arc**: `DJ Intro(2) → Intro Swell(3) → Verse 1(5) → Pre-Chorus(7) → Chorus(8) → Drop(9) → Breakdown(4) → Verse 2(6) → Build-up(8) → Second Drop(9) → Bridge(4) → Final Build(9) → Final Drop(10) → DJ Outro(3)`

## 7. Phonetic Polish for AI Singers

AI vocal synthesis reads literal spelling more reliably than punctuation shorthand:

1. **Spell out numbers & clock times**:
   - `24/7` → `twenty four seven`
   - `24/7/365` → `twenty four seven three sixty five`
   - `3am` → `three a m` (same for `1am`–`12am`, `1pm`–`11pm`)
   - `1/2` → `one half`
2. **Space out short ALL-CAPS acronyms letter-by-letter** (leaving `[Section Tags]` untouched):
   - `AI` → `A I`, `ATM` → `A T M`, `BPM` → `B P M`, `CD` → `C D`, `CPU` → `C P U`, `DVD` → `D V D`, `FM` → `F M`, `GPS` → `G P S`, `HD` → `H D`, `ID` → `I D`, `LCD` → `L C D`, `LED` → `L E D`, `PC` → `P C`, `SMS` → `S M S`, `TV` → `T V`, `USB` → `U S B`, `VIP` → `V I P`, `VR` → `V R`, `VHS` → `V H S`
3. **Control vocal intensity with casing**: Avoid casual ALL-CAPS emphasis words unless intentional shouting is wanted. Keep normal sentence casing for relaxed, controlled vocals.
4. **Sustained vowels**: Stretch held notes with hyphens or repeated letters (`sta-a-ay`, `lo-o-ove`, `frrree`).
5. **Dramatic breath pauses**: Use ellipses inside a line (`I... need... you`).
6. **Zero Yoda-speak**: Never invert natural word order just to land a rhyme—choose a fresher rhyme instead.

## 8. Anti-Cliché & Anti-Slop Bans (Multilingual)

### Banned AI-Slop Imagery
- **Hard ban in sung lyrics**: `neon` / `néon` / `neón`.
- **Avoid AI-slop clichés in both Style & Lyrics**: `circuits`, `digital void`, `ghost in the machine`, `hologram`, `velvet sky`, `matrix`.
- **Rule**: Never "fix" a cliché by swapping in a single predictable synonym (e.g., turning `neon` into `electric`). Rewrite the line with a concrete image from the actual story.

### Stock Phrases to Avoid Accumulating
- **English**: `party all night`, `feel the beat`, `hands in the air`, `we own the night`, `lose control`, `dancing tonight`, `turn it up`, `let the music take you`, `under the stars`, `feel alive`, `into the night`, `can't stop moving`, `dance the night away`, `raise your hands`, `feel the music`, `lose yourself`, `the night is young`, `one more time`, `tonight we are free`, `electric love`, `neon dreams`, `city of lights`, `heartbeat of the night`, `shadows dancing`, `ignite the spark`
- **Portuguese**: `sentir a batida`, `mãos para o alto`, `dançar a noite inteira`, `perder o controle`, `nosso amor é infinito`, `você é meu destino`, `meu coração dispara`, `teu abraço é meu lugar`
- **Spanish**: `siente el ritmo`, `manos arriba`, `bailar toda la noche`, `perder el control`, `nuestro amor es infinito`, `eres mi destino`, `mi corazón se acelera`, `tu abrazo es mi hogar`

### Multilingual Contract
- Title and every sung line must remain **100% in the requested lyric language** (no accidental English count-ins like `"one, two, three"` inside a Portuguese or Spanish song unless explicitly asked for bilingual code-switching).
- Always keep `Style`, `Exclude Styles`, `[Section Tags]`, and `(performance cues)` in English.
- Add the target vocal language cue to `Style` for non-English vocal songs (`Brazilian Portuguese vocals`, `Spanish vocals`, `French vocals`, `Arabic vocals`, etc.).
